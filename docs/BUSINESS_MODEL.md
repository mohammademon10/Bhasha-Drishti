# Business Model & Global Readiness

## Value proposition
A Bangla-first, bounded communication-support layer for Deaf/BdSL signers and hearing communication partners. It is not a replacement for human interpreters.

## Customer segments
Inclusive schools, universities, hospitals/clinics for reception-only interactions, banks/retail customer desks, NGOs and public-service counters.

## Revenue hypotheses
- Institutional annual licence per campus/branch.
- Usage-based accessibility API.
- Training/onboarding for staff.
- NGO/CSR-sponsored deployment.
- Free basic access; paid admin/custom phrase packs/support.
These are hypotheses, not validated prices. Avoid charging individual Deaf users for essential accessibility features in early pilots.

## Validation
Interview 5–8 Deaf/BdSL users, 3–5 teachers/interpreters and 3 institutional administrators. Ask which interactions matter, acceptable accuracy/latency, privacy concerns, clip-vs-avatar preference and who owns budget. Record consent; do not invent outcomes.

## Pilot
4–6 weeks with one school/NGO/university accessibility office. Co-design vocabulary, train staff, measure task completion, time-to-understanding, correction rate and user satisfaction; human help remains available.

## Global readiness
Separate language-specific ontology, labels, clips, text normalization and models from shared infrastructure. BdSL does not automatically transfer to ASL/BSL/ISL; every sign language needs local linguistic and community validation.

## NRB collaboration
Potential areas: accessibility UX, ML review, deployment and research connections. Claim collaboration only if it actually happened and is documented.

## KPIs
Validated pilot interactions, task completion, median communication time, usefulness/trust, correction/abstention rate, institutional willingness to pilot/pay, cost per deployment.
